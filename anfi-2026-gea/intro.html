Generated items can't be calibrated the old way.

Classical calibration

Fixed item bank. Each item gets difficulty and discrimination estimates from 50–200 real responses before use.

LLM-generated assessment

Every student can get a different item. The rubric changes each semester. There is no time to collect 50 responses per item.

The bootstrapping problem: you can't validate without student data, and you can't deploy without validation. Simulated students fill the gap.

Prior work reports LLM-simulated respondents approximating human item calibration with correlations above 0.89 (Liu 2025). The catch is on the next slide.

Four things simulated students can do. We focus on two.

Teacher training

Practise diagnosing and responding to common misconceptions on a student who never gets tired.

Item design

Both of today's uses share one question: when the twin gets a score, is the score right?

Simulated students are cheap, repeatable and available before any real student has seen the item.

One LLM writes the item, plays the student, and grades the answer.

It plays weak students badly

Expert knowledge leaks through, and none of 11 LLMs matched real student score distributions.

It disagrees with itself

Even proprietary models score inconsistently at temperature above 0.

Wang 2024

The result can look consistent and still be wrong. So: when the model generates at skill x, does its own scoring recover x?

Adaptive drift is the gap between what we asked for and what we scored.

E[ score( generate(x) ) ] ≈ xGenerative-Evaluative Agreement (GEA)

Small drift near the threshold sends a student down the wrong path.

Four levers narrow the gap. You'll pull some of them in a minute.

Break the rubric into skills

Score each skill separately, with a clear line between "has it" and "doesn't", then average. The paper's main proposal.

Use a different model to score

A model rates its own writing higher. Let another one grade.

Say which misconceptions are active

"Act like a beginner" leaks expertise. "Never validates input, believes class variables are per-object" doesn't.

Check against a few real students

10–20 real answers anchor the simulated numbers.
