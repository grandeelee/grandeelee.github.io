Build five simulated students. Then catch your chatbot marking them wrong.

Work in pairs with any free web chatbot (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot). Pick a subject you actually teach. You'll see adaptive drift with your own eyes, then try one fix.

15:00

1

Set up your task

2 min

Pick a subject, or write your own task. The page turns what you type into ready-to-paste prompts.

Maker
Holds this page. Runs the twin generator and knows the secret skill levels.
Judge
Uses a separate chat window. Never sees the levels. Marks the answers. Working alone? Use two chats and look away from the levels.
2

Maker: make five twins

3 min

The page has dealt each twin a secret level (0–100) for every skill in your rubric, so a twin can be strong on one skill and weak on another. The overall level is the average. Two twins sit close to the pass mark of 50, because that's where drift hurts most. Paste the prompt into a fresh chat.

Check the chatbot kept the labels P–T and left the levels out of the text. Send only the answers to the Judge.

3

Judge: mark them cold

3 min

In a new chat, paste this prompt. The Judge has never seen the levels, which is exactly how a real assessment bot works.

4

See the gap

2 min

intended levelfirst scorescore after your fix

Pass mark θ = 50 (dashed line). A student is misrouted when their intended level and their score fall on different sides of it. With five students this is a feel, not a result. The paper used 7,788 paired observations.

5

Tighten the ruler

4 min

Pick one lever (or deal one at random) and re-run. The levels stay the same, so you can compare directly. Different pairs can pick different levers.

6

Debrief

1 min + discussion
  • Which student drifted the most? Was it a weak or a strong one?
  • Which of your rubric skills would you trust the chatbot to mark alone? Which needs a person?
  • If this bot routed real students at a pass mark of 50, who would you check by hand?
